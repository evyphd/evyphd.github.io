Featured research

When making AI “fair” can create unintended harm

New peer-reviewed research in Marketing Science examines what happens when platforms impose fairness constraints on AI recommendations while consumers strategically decide whether to disclose their identities.

Published online August 14, 2026

My Fair AI: Accuracy Parity and Strategic Identity Disclosure in AI Content Recommendation

AI platforms increasingly try to deliver comparable recommendation quality across demographic groups. Our research shows that these policies can change both consumer disclosure behavior and firms’ incentives to invest in AI. Under some conditions, a policy designed to protect disadvantaged groups can unintentionally make those consumers worse off.

Evelyn O. Smith, Jeffrey D. Shulman & Antino Kim · Marketing Science · Supported in part by the University of Kentucky CURATE Research Support Program.

About

Research at the intersection of AI, business & people

I am an Assistant Professor of Marketing in the Gatton College of Business and Economics at the University of Kentucky. My research uses analytical, mathematical, game-theoretic, and empirical approaches to understand emerging technologies and the strategic behavior they create.

I am particularly interested in situations where technology produces unexpected effects: a fairness policy that changes incentives, a recommendation system that serves some groups better than others, or an AI conversation that changes how people engage with difficult ideas.

Before entering academia, I worked as an Artificial Intelligence Product Marketing Researcher at Microsoft. I earned my Ph.D. in Marketing from the University of Washington’s Foster School of Business, with specialization in game theory and minors in economics and statistics.
